# Water authority questions in UM_2025-0004

This is a research map, not legal advice. Its purpose is to keep separate the
land-use permit, public-water-system permit, district powers, emergency trigger,
funding scope, and actual delivery operation.

## Preliminary answer

The County's permit condition does not, by itself, establish that MUSD may
provide water throughout the area drawn by the Planning Commission.

MUSD operates active public water system **CA2300584**, classified by the State
as a nontransient noncommunity groundwater system. That establishes regulated
operation of a school water system. It does not tell us the operative terms of
MUSD's November 26, 2025 amended domestic water supply permit, including
authorized customers, tanker filling, dispensing points, or emergency delivery.

[Water Code §13198](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=13198.&lawCode=WAT)
does not independently answer that question. It defines:

- a qualifying "drought scenario"; and
- forms of "interim or immediate relief," including hauled water, community
  tanks, emergency interties, wells, and permanent connections.

The County used §13198(a) as an **event trigger** for Condition 18. The section
does not enlarge a drinking-water permit, create a district, or assign a service
territory.

## Joint powers are possible, but not yet demonstrated

The structured analysis is in
[`authority-chain.yaml`](authority-chain.yaml), with a readable explanation in
[`water-authority-chain.md`](water-authority-chain.md).

Water Code §10707 expressly allows a joint-powers agreement for the special
Mendocino groundwater program. Government Code §6502, however, requires a power
common to the contracting agencies. The November 2024 MUSD–MCCSD MOU proves
cooperative implementation and allocates ownership, operation, CEQA, and grant
control. It does not on its face create a separate JPA, identify a common power
being jointly exercised, or replace DDW and LAFCo approvals. Whether it was
intended and approved as a non-entity joint-powers agreement remains an
evidence question.

MCCSD also should not be treated as legally “non-water.” The 1985 Measure A,
LAFCo's 2005 service inventory, its 2020 MSR, and its 2022 confirmation letter
establish active water and groundwater powers. The narrower fact is that MCCSD
does not currently operate a municipal central drinking-water system. LAFCo
identified that system as a new or different class of service requiring
activation.

Water Code §10717 adds a transition problem: MCCSD's special Part 2.7
groundwater authority ends when a municipal central water system is completed
and implemented to serve inhabitants within MCCSD. Emergency hauled water may
not satisfy that description, but a later conversion to routine community
supply could require a specific legal determination.

The official published opinion in *Gomes v. Mendocino City Community Services
District* (2019) 35 Cal.App.5th 249 supplies the controlling procedural history.
It quotes AB 786 author Dan Hauser's signing letter, describes Ordinance 90-1,
and holds that Ordinance 07-1, Resolution 200, and Ordinance 07-4 were void
because MCCSD had not followed Water Code §§10703-10706. MCCSD later readopted
its groundwater program through Ordinance 2020-01. The opinion does not replace
the still-missing signed early ordinances.

## How the emergency area changed

The June 2023 Final Subsequent MND repeatedly describes the project as serving
the emergency water needs of **MCCSD customers**. It says DWR Agreement
4600014624 identifies MUSD and MCCSD as implementing agencies and funds storage,
wells, and a connection to MUSD's distribution system.

The broader "emergency drought water service area" appeared later in the County
permit process. According to the Coastal Commission's April 2025 appeal report,
the December 5, 2024 discussion selected:

1. the existing MCCSD service area; plus
2. territory extending to the Slaughterhouse Gulch watershed boundary.

MUSD supplied the resulting Exhibit I before the December 19 hearing. The map
did not show parcel boundaries. The best current characterization is therefore
a **County-permit eligibility area**, not a demonstrated:

- DDW-authorized MUSD service territory;
- MCCSD annexation;
- MCCSD sphere amendment;
- LAFCo-approved out-of-area service area; or
- grant boundary.

Those may overlap, but the corpus must not merge them.

## What the certified Town LCP says about MCCSD permits

The Coastal Commission's May 26, 2017 certification report supplies a decisive
distinction. At PDF page 82 it states:

> The ordinances establish the District's criteria in determining when a
> groundwater extraction permit would be required. However, the County has
> never applied for an LCP amendment to incorporate any of the MCCSD
> groundwater management extraction permit provisions into the certified Town
> LCP.

This does **not** mean MCCSD groundwater management is irrelevant to coastal
permitting. The same report says the certified Town Plan uses MCCSD or County
Health Officer determinations in applying hydrological-study requirements, and
the 2017 amendment deliberately aligned a limited-increase threshold with
MCCSD's demand standards. The important legal-data distinction is:

- MCCSD's separate extraction-permit ordinances were not themselves made part
  of the certified LCP;
- the certified LCP contains its own groundwater and proof-of-water rules,
  some of which use MCCSD findings or standards; and
- a County planner's operational reliance on an MCCSD permit is evidence of
  interagency practice, not proof that the whole MCCSD ordinance was
  incorporated into the LCP.

A separate 2018-2022 coastal-permit record recovered from public request
**22-583** shows that practice in operation. County staff postponed a hearing
while MCCSD's Stage 4 shortage prevented a Groundwater Extraction Permit, then
proceeded after MCCSD approved the permit, hydrological review, daily allotment,
and sewer rights. This is useful historical evidence, but it does not erase the
Commission's express non-incorporation finding.

## What the prior hauled-water record shows

County public-record request **23-445** released an internal water-hauling
recap for September 2021 through August or September 2022. It reports:

- 1,291,100 gallons hauled from Ukiah to Fort Bragg;
- 1,291,402 gallons delivered from Fort Bragg to residential customers;
- 414,500 gallons assigned to Mendocino; and
- 549,900 gallons assigned to Fort Bragg.

This is useful evidence of actual emergency delivery scale and destinations,
but it does not identify individual recipients, eligibility rules, receiving
tanks, or legal service territory. Its destination table totals 1,294,402
gallons—3,000 gallons more than the stated residential grand total—so the
source contains an unresolved internal arithmetic discrepancy.

## What the principal statutes control

| Authority | What it contributes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Water Code §13198 | Drought trigger and forms of immediate relief | MUSD service authority or a territorial expansion |
| H&S Code §§116525, 116530–116550 | DDW application, investigation, permit conditions, and amended-permit requirements | County land-use approval or district boundaries |
| H&S Code §116555 | Reliable potable supply, standards, backflow, and operator duties | Eligibility for service outside the permitted operation |
| H&S Code §116682 | State-ordered consolidation or extension after a specific process | Authority here unless the State actually invoked it |
| Gov. Code §61100(a) | MCCSD water power within its boundary | A central system outside the boundary |
| Gov. Code §56133 | LAFCo approval for applicable city/district out-of-area service | MUSD authority; school districts are excluded from the CKH definition of district |
| Gov. Code §§56824.10–56824.14 | Activation of a latent or new district service | Automatic activation through a County permit |
| Education Code §§35160–35160.1 | Broad school-district powers consistent with law and school purposes | An express answer on continuing area-wide retail water supply |
| PRC §30600(a) | Coastal approval remains required | Substitution of a CDP for other permits required by law |

## Why MCCSD is not simply supplying the water

The 2020 LAFCo municipal-service review says MCCSD has no municipal central
water system. It manages groundwater and has some water-related functions, but
a central system is treated as a latent or new class of service requiring LAFCo
approval. Its sphere is coterminous with its approximately one-square-mile
boundary.

An MCCSD model could therefore require some combination of:

- activation of a latent or new water service;
- a funded service plan;
- a sphere amendment and annexation for expanded territory; or
- LAFCo approval under Government Code §56133 for applicable out-of-area
  service.

The public LAFCo docket searched from 2020 through July 2026 contains no
project-specific application, boundary change, latent-power proceeding,
out-of-area-service approval, or determination for this MUSD/MCCSD project.
That is a bounded negative finding: informal correspondence, pre-application
consultation, and internal files may still exist. Government Code §56133
applies to cities and covered districts; because §56036 excludes school
districts, it is not by itself a basis to require MUSD, acting in its own
capacity, to obtain LAFCo approval. It could become relevant if MCCSD is the
entity providing service outside its boundary.

The present project avoided that institutional route. The wells, treatment, and
storage are on MUSD property; MUSD already operates a DDW-permitted system; and
water is contemplated as hauled emergency supply rather than a new MCCSD pipe
network. That may explain the chosen design, but it does not prove that all
institutional-authority questions were resolved.

## What the signed 2024 MOU establishes

The agreement is the third materially different version recovered from public
meeting records. The September 2022 version assigned ownership and operation of
the tank and wells to MCCSD and contemplated MCCSD emergency-water service to
eligible residential and commercial customers. The April 2023 replacement
left equitable access to later mutual determination and assigned operation of
the new wells to MCCSD. The November 2024 replacement instead assigns all
water-system ownership and operation to MUSD and narrows non-school/non-fire
access to declared drought emergencies when neighboring districts cannot
supply hauled water.

The locally OCRed November 2024 MUSD-MCCSD MOU says:

- MUSD will own all water-system assets and bear operating and maintenance
  costs.
- Access by users other than MUSD and fire departments is limited to a declared
  drought emergency when neighboring water districts cannot supply hauled
  water.
- Only water exceeding MUSD demand and fire-protection needs is available.
- People whose wells go dry must apply for temporary emergency water through a
  process to be determined jointly by MUSD and MCCSD.
- MCCSD controls changes to the UMBDR-funded improvements, subject to DWR
  approval.
- MUSD controls changes to DWSRF-funded improvements, subject to State Water
  Board Division of Financial Assistance approval.
- MUSD remains CEQA lead and must complete the studies, design, and permitting.

The MOU does **not** identify the later Slaughterhouse Gulch watershed boundary,
amend MUSD's DDW permit, activate an MCCSD central-water service, or show LAFCo
approval. It is an operating and funding allocation agreement, not a complete
service-authority instrument.

## What the executed funding agreements establish

The June 2023 MUSD Response to Comments publicly reproduces two executed
agreements that answer the original funding-scope question but are not the
complete current agreement history.

**DWR Agreement 4600014624, Amendment 1** provides up to **$4,932,000** to
MCCSD. Its work plan covers a 500,000-gallon emergency tank, an intertie to
MUSD's system, and up to ten wells. MCCSD is the grantee and MUSD is an
implementing/local sponsoring agency. The agreement requires MCCSD to ensure
operation and maintenance for the project's useful life; transferring that
responsibility requires DWR's written approval. It does not contain a
parcel-level beneficiary polygon or adopt the County's later Slaughterhouse
Gulch boundary.

**State Water Board Agreement D2202005**, project **2300584-001C**, provides
**$4,500,000 in principal forgiveness** to MUSD. Its original scope is narrower:
about 115,000 gallons of replacement storage, work on three wells, treatment
and monitoring improvements, piping, a hydrant, generator, fencing, and access.
The agreement requires MUSD to own, operate, and maintain the funded project
and restricts transfer or substantial discontinuance without DFA approval.

The State Water Board's June 20, 2023 comment letter says D2202005 does not
match the expanded environmental project and directs MUSD to request a funding
project update. No approval or revised scope has been found online. A March
2026 MUSD report also gives an October 30, 2026 construction deadline, while
the original agreement uses July 1, 2026.

DWR's March 5, 2026 letter separately refers to the **latest amendment** to
4600014624 and a March 31, 2026 work deadline. Amendment 1 used March 31, 2025,
so at least one later DWR amendment exists but has not been located.

These version gaps matter because the November 2024 MOU shifted ownership and
all operating costs to MUSD. The captured agreements require regulator approval
for material scope, ownership, disposition, or O&M changes; the corresponding
DWR and DFA approvals remain missing.

## Documents that will answer the question

The highest-value record is the **November 26, 2025 amended domestic water
supply permit for CA2300584**, with its application, technical report,
engineering report, approved plans, and conditions. It should show whether DDW
reviewed:

- emergency public distribution;
- permitted customers or service scope;
- tanker or hydrant filling;
- storage ownership and operating responsibility;
- water-quality and backflow controls; and
- the County-created emergency area.

The next records are:

1. MUSD's immediately preceding DDW permit.
2. DWR amendments after public Amendment 1, including the "latest amendment"
   cited in March 2026.
3. D2202005 amendments and MUSD's funding-project update.
4. County Exhibit I in GIS or parcel-overlay form.
5. The final signed 2024 and 2026 condition sets and their redlines.
6. Any LAFCo inquiry or determination about latent powers, annexation, or
   out-of-area service.
7. Operating procedures and agreements with licensed water haulers.
