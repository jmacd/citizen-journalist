

As noted in the memo, the only zoning types that currently allow off-site solar farms are AG, RL, FL, TP and I.  In my mind, the Industrial district is off the table due to the significant differences in uses between that and the existing area.  TPZ would require a timber harvest plan and approval of the State Board of Forestry so that’s also not realistic, which leaves us with AG, RL or FL.  I had recommended FL simply due to the proximity of adjacent TPZ lands to the south, and staff could easily support an FL zoning on that site as a ‘buffer’ to the TPZ.  I’ll note here your concern about preventing future residential development; while limiting development is not a goal of the LCP, well-sited development is, and there are additional policies that recommend limited development adjacent to TPZ lands in order to reduce development pressure on those resources.  So in this case, FL would likely be the easiest proposal for staff to support but ultimately any of those three zones would accomplish the goal.  However, due to the density limitations of those zonings, a reduction in potential homesite development will be a side-effect of any of them.



Staff recommendations for rezones are always a bit tricky, because zoning is a policy decision.  I was glad to see that you’ve included Supervisor Norvell in your outreach, and encourage you to have a dialogue with him regarding future uses of the site.  Coastal Commission review is also a significant factor, both in time required for approving the requested updates as well as any concerns they may have.  A Coastal Development Use Permit would also be required for construction of the array, but that could come at a later date if a developer is secured.

I was able to dig up my correspondence with the CCC from last year, which I’ve attached here.  I’ve also attached Ordinance 3857 which is referenced in that email chain.  For context, “IP” as used in the emails means ‘implementation plan’, and refers to the County’s Division II zoning code.



As the General Plan (and by extension the LCP) is the County’s governing document with regard to land uses, it will take precedence over the zoning district, which is how I arrived at that conclusion in the PAC memo.  As you noted, there is a possibility that a correction or modification to either the LCP or IP could be included as part of the ongoing LCP update, and the Department is certainly willing to explore that discussion.  I do think that a zone change is ultimately a slightly easier lift when compared to amending the policy, because it would allow the Department to sidestep the analysis that would be required if we wanted to allow commercial solar arrays in every RR zone along the coast. But I also understand the desire to keep residential zonings available, and while there wouldn’t be a huge change in development potential in this case, there would be a difference; the current lot is 50± acres and zoned for one unit per 10 acres.  Assuming a successful subdivision into 5 lots, we’d be looking at a maximum of 10 dwelling units (pending water and septic availability, and not including JADUs) under the current RR-10 zoning.  By contrast, the FL zone would reduce that potential to just 2 units.  Since at least one of the subdivision lots would likely be needed for the array itself, I don’t think maximum buildout of 10 units is realistic so we’re probably looking at something closer to 2 units versus 8 units.  Still a loss of potential housing, but likely not as significant as the benefits from additional renewable energy would be.

Here’s some additional info/observations:
The Commission’s findings for certifying the IP acknowledge that the Zoning Districts
mirror the LUP categories. The LUP includes off-site alternative energy facilities as a
conditional permitted use for lands designated I, AG and RL, but not RR. So, the
controlling land uses of the land use plan do not list off-site energy facilities as an
allowable use in RR classifications.
Ordinance 3857 that was attached in Russ’ email was associated with Minor
Amendment 1-92 which added fire and police services as conditional uses in the RR
district. Even though the LCPA included other changes to the IP, it didn’t add any
other conditional uses in the RR district. So, even though as Russ points out, it
illustrates a point in time where the alternative energy use type was listed in the LCP,
it isn’t clear whether that specific use was ever certified in the RR district.

Good morning State team.
I was working on a Pre-Application Conference (PAC) memo yesterday and discovered an inconsistency in
our Coastal documents that we’re hoping you may have some additional information on. For context, the
PAC is for a proposal to create a 2 megawatt commercial solar array in the Caspar area. APN 118-090-12,
at 44661 Caspar Orchard Road. It’s the lot right near the southeast corner of Highway 1 and Fern Creek

Road in Caspar.
The project type would likely be classified as Alternative Energy Facilities: Off-site, a Coastal Civic Use
type. The site is zoned RR-10. MCC §20.376.015(B) for the RR district lists Alternative Energy Facilities:
Off-site as a conditional use for the RR zone. However, §20.320.020 provides a definition for that use type
and indicates that off-site use “...shall be permitted as a conditional use in AG, RL, FL, TP and I
Districts”, notably omitting RR zones. Additionally, LCP Policy 3.11-12 discusses alternative energy
facilities, and says:
“For off-site use, alternative energy facilities shall be considered as a conditional use in the
Agriculture, Forest Lands, Industrial Land and Range Land categories.”
To compound things, we discovered Ordinance 3857 (attached) which amended Chapter 20.376 for the
Coastal RR classification in 1993. That ordinance clearly includes Alternative Energy Facilities: Off-site
as a conditional use, and it appears that this inconsistency has been on the books since at least 1987.
As the original LCP, the ordinance update in 1993, and any subsequent updates would have required State
approval, I was hoping there may be some notes in your files that could shed light on this. It sounds like
we’ll need to add this correction into our pending LCP update, but more pressingly, I want to be correct in
the information I’m giving to the PAC applicants. If it turns out the site would require an LCP amendment,
then it’s probably not going to be worth their investment.
Appreciate any info you have, and let me know if you have any questions.
