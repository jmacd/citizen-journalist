# Water authority and joint-powers chain

This is a research framework, not a legal opinion. It prevents an agreement,
permit, funding award, or agency label from being treated as if it supplied all
other necessary authority.

## The short answer

MCCSD is not best described as a “non-water district.” It is a community
services district with voter-approved and LAFCo-confirmed **active water and
groundwater-management powers**. It does not currently operate a municipal
central drinking-water system. MUSD, meanwhile, owns and operates the
DDW-regulated school public water system **CA2300584**, but being a public water
system is a regulatory status—not, by itself, a grant of general-purpose
municipal water power.

The combined project therefore overlays distinct roles:

| Actor | Role in this project | Approval or power it cannot replace |
|---|---|---|
| DDW | Permits and regulates CA2300584 | MCCSD/LAFCo service authority or County land-use approval |
| DFA | Controls DWSRF financing scope | DDW operating permit |
| DWR | Controls UMBDR grant scope | Drinking-water or district authority |
| MUSD | Owns and operates the public water system and project assets | MCCSD groundwater regulation or unproven area-wide retail authority |
| MCCSD | Regulates aquifer extraction and holds CSD water powers | DDW operation of CA2300584 |
| LAFCo | Determines MCCSD boundaries and applicable service activation | DDW permit or school-district power |
| County/Coastal Commission | Controls land-use and coastal development | DDW, LAFCo, or underlying agency power |

## The joint-powers question

Water Code §10707 expressly allows an agency administering this special
Mendocino groundwater program to enter a joint-powers agreement under
Government Code §§6500 et seq. That route is legally available, but it does not
mean every interagency MOU is automatically a JPA.

Government Code §6502 requires a power **common to the contracting parties**.
Section 6503 requires the agreement to state the purpose or power and how it
will be exercised. A separate entity is optional; when one is created,
additional notice, filing, treasury, audit, and accountability requirements
apply. Calling a document an “MOU” is not dispositive, but neither is public
agency cooperation enough by itself.

MCCSD's November 25, 2024 action minutes record separate 4–0 votes, with one
director absent, to rescind the April 20, 2023 MOU and adopt the revised MOU.
The revised instrument was therefore not merely a staff draft. It:

- combines two funding streams into one construction project;
- makes MUSD owner and operator of all water-system assets;
- makes MUSD CEQA lead;
- subjects project wells to MCCSD Ordinance 2020-01;
- reserves control of each grant-funded component to its grant recipient and
  grantor; and
- postpones the detailed emergency-water application process.

It does not create a separate entity, cite the Joint Exercise of Powers Act,
identify a common power being jointly exercised, establish joint governance,
or amend DDW or LAFCo approvals. The safest current finding is therefore:
**cooperative implementation is proven; JPA status and any transfer or joint
exercise of governmental power are not.**

The historical agreements are separate chains. County-MCCSD Agreement 90-113
transferred groundwater-extraction regulation within MCCSD to the District to
the extent authorized by law. The 1997 MUSD-MCCSD MOU and Joint Resolution
97-1 concern tertiary recycled water for school athletic-field irrigation.
Neither is the predecessor potable-project MOU: that chain runs from the
September 2022 agreement, through the April 20, 2023 replacement, to the
superseding November 2024 agreement.

Those supersessions materially narrowed and reassigned the project. The 2022
MOU assigned ownership and operation of the tank and wells to MCCSD and
contemplated MCCSD emergency service to eligible residential and commercial
customers. The 2023 MOU left “equitable access” to later mutual determination
and assigned MCCSD operation and maintenance of the new wells plus
proportionate system costs. The 2024 MOU instead makes MUSD owner and operator
of all water-system assets and limits non-school/non-fire access to declared
drought emergencies when neighboring districts cannot provide hauled water.
The exact operating and application rules remain deferred.

## MCCSD’s two different water roles

MCCSD has overlapping but legally distinct authority:

1. **General CSD water power.** Measure A and Government Code §61100(a) support
   water for beneficial uses within the district.
2. **Special groundwater-management power.** Water Code §§10700–10717 applies
   only inside MCCSD’s existing boundary and supports extraction permits,
   rates or assessments after required procedures, and replenishment powers.

LAFCo’s 2020 MSR drew a further distinction. Groundwater management, recycled
water, and limited stored well water were existing classes of service. A
municipal central water system—and some replenishment activity—would be a new
or different class requiring LAFCo activation.

No project-specific application, boundary change, latent-power proceeding,
out-of-area-service approval, or determination appears in the public LAFCo
docket searched from 2020 through July 2026. This does not exclude informal
correspondence, pre-application consultation, or internal files. Government
Code §56133 does not itself apply to MUSD acting in its own capacity because
§56036 excludes school districts from the Act's definition of “district.” It
could apply if MCCSD is the entity providing service beyond its boundary.

This makes Water Code §10717 especially important. It says the special Part 2.7
authority ends when a municipal central water system is completed and
implemented to supply inhabitants within MCCSD. The current emergency hauled
water project may not meet that description, but the statute creates a real
transition question if the project evolves into regular community supply.
Triggering §10717 would end the **special Part 2.7 powers**; it would not
necessarily erase MCCSD’s separate Government Code §61100 water power.

## A reusable reasoning chain

For each proposed action, ask in this order:

1. **Act:** Is this aquifer management, extraction permitting, well
   construction, PWS operation, tanker filling, customer eligibility, or
   central retail service?
2. **Holder:** Which agency possesses that power before any agreement?
3. **Territory and customers:** Which boundary and population does that power
   cover?
4. **Instrument:** Is cooperation a service contract, MOU, non-entity JPA, or
   separate-entity JPA?
5. **Independent approvals:** What must DDW, LAFCo, County/Coastal, RWQCB,
   DWR, or DFA separately approve?
6. **Transition:** Would implementation trigger Water Code §10717 or a new
   LAFCo service class?

The missing 2025 DDW permit and any nonpublic project-specific LAFCo
correspondence remain the most important evidence. They should show whether the agencies treated
emergency community distribution as an amendment to a school system, an
MCCSD service, a jointly exercised power, or only a tightly limited emergency
operation.
